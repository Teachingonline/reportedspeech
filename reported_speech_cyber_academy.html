MODULE 01 // CORE TRANSMISSION

What is Reported Speech?

We use Reported Speech (also called Indirect Speech) to communicate what someone else said without using their exact words or quotation marks. Because we are reporting past words at a later moment, we usually shift pronouns, verb tenses (one step back into the past), and time/place words.

GOLDEN RULE:

Direct: Exact words inside "..."
Reported: No quotes + Reporting Verb + Backshifted Tense

1. DIRECT SPEECH (Original Quote)

Captain Alex said, “I am testing my laser shield here today.”

2. REPORTED SPEECH (Transformed)

Captain Alex said (that) he was testing his laser shield there that day.

SAID (No Personal Object)

Use said when you do not mention who the listener was.

Maya said (that) she liked synthwave music.

TOLD + OBJECT (Listener Required)

Use told + person/pronoun when you specify who received the message.

Maya told me (that) she liked synthwave music.

MODULE 03 // SPACE-TIME COORDINATES

Time & Place Shift Decoder

When the time or location changes between the original speech and the report, these words transform:

now ➔ then / at that moment
today ➔ that day
tonight ➔ that night
tomorrow ➔ the next / following day
yesterday ➔ the day before
next week ➔ the following week
here ➔ there
this / these ➔ that / those
ago ➔ before / earlier
MODULE 04 // INTERROGATIVE PROTOCOL

Reporting Questions

In reported questions, do NOT use question word order (no do/does/did auxiliary, and no question mark ?). Use affirmative Subject + Verb order!

A. YES / NO QUESTIONS → Use IF or WHETHER

Direct: “Do you speak Binary?”

Reported: He asked me if I spoke Binary.

B. WH- QUESTIONS → Keep the WH- Word

Direct: “Where are you going now?”

Reported: She asked where I was going then.

MODULE 05 // IMPERATIVE DIRECTIVES

Commands & Requests

We do not backshift tenses in imperatives. Instead, use told / asked + object + (not) to + infinitive.

A. POSITIVE COMMAND / REQUEST

Direct: “Activate the hyperdrive!”

Reported: The pilot told us to activate the hyperdrive.

B. NEGATIVE COMMAND (DON'T → NOT TO)

Direct: “Please don't touch the red button.”

Reported: She asked me not to touch the red button.
